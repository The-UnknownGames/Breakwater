# BREAKWATER — Game Specification

A realistic storm salvage and sea rescue game. You run a small salvage-and-rescue outfit on a rough northern coast. When the weather turns, the sea fills with work: towing disabled vessels, recovering spilled cargo, and pulling people out of the water before the cold does.

**Core idea: the worse the weather, the more work there is, and the more it pays. It is also the most likely time to lose your boat.**

You are building this game **fully autonomously**. The human will not answer questions.

6. **Git:** commit at the end of every milestone and after any significant working change. If a remote named `origin` exists, push after every commit. Never push with `--force`. If a change makes things worse, `git revert` it.
7. **Code style:** ES modules, one statement per line, files under ~400 lines, and gameplay numbers in `src/config/` files, not scattered through logic.
8. **Context hygiene:** do one milestone (or one milestone sub-step) per session. Keep `docs/PROGRESS.md` under ~150 lines: current milestone, NEXT steps, known issues, Decisions, and the polish backlog. Move finished history into `docs/CHANGELOG.md`.

## 1. Design goals

1. **The boat feels real.** It has weight, momentum, and inertia. It pitches over swells, heels in wind, slams off wave crests, and steers badly at low speed without prop wash. The physics is the game.
2. **The sea is the antagonist.** Weather changes everything about the same job. A calm-day tow is a routine chore; the same tow in a gale is a fight.
3. **Hard choices under pressure.** Take the high-paying storm mayday or wait for the weather to clear? Cut a tow line to save your own boat? Go back for the last survivor with fuel running low?
4. **Photographic, not stylized.** It should look like storm footage from a coast guard cutter, not a cartoon and not a synthwave poster.
5. **Readable under chaos.** Even in a night storm, the player always knows their heading, speed, tow tension, flooding, and objective.

### 2.4 Rendering (Three.js, `WebGLRenderer`)
- **Color:** `SRGBColorSpace`, `ACESFilmicToneMapping`, with physically based materials (`MeshStandardMaterial`/`MeshPhysicalMaterial`) everywhere.
- **Post-processing** (EffectComposer):
  - SMAA or FXAA.
  - Subtle bloom *only* above a high threshold, so just the sun, lightning, and the searchlight core. Keep it realistic; never glowing UI.
  - Gentle vignette.
  - A color-grading pass that shifts with weather.
  - Every pass can be toggled by the quality setting.
- **Shadows:** a cascaded or tightly-fit directional shadow following the player's boat.
- **Sky:** the Three.js `Sky` (Preetham/Hosek) for sun and atmosphere, plus a procedural cloud layer (fbm noise on a dome or plane) whose coverage and darkness follow the weather. Render the sky to a cube camera every few seconds to make the ocean reflection environment map.
- **Fog:** height-based exponential fog with density driven by weather. In storms, visibility drops to about 400 m, so radar and lights matter.

### 2.5 Ocean rendering (the most important visual system)
- **Mesh:** a camera-following grid that's dense near the camera and coarser farther out (either nested rings or a projected grid), with no visible edge. Snap it to grid steps so it doesn't swim.
- **Waves:** a sum of 12–16 Gerstner waves in the vertex shader, driven by the **same wave list** the physics uses (section 3.2). The shader and the physics must never disagree about where the surface is.
- **Detail normals:** two scrolling procedural normal maps, generated at startup, for small-scale ripples, with strength that increases with wind.
- **Shading:**
  - Fresnel blend between the sky reflection and the water body color (deep → mid).
  - Subsurface scattering color on crests facing away from the sun.
  - A sun specular highlight.
  - Darker water in deep areas; lighter, greener water over shallows (from the depth map).
- **Foam:**
  - **Crest foam** from the wave Jacobian or steepness, increasing with sea state.
  - **Dynamic foam:** a foam render target (about 256×256, covering 200 m around the player, following the player) where wakes, hull contact, slams, and breaking surf are painted each frame and fade over time.
  - **Shore foam** where the water depth is under 1.5 m.
- **Spray:** instanced particles for bow slams, hull spray at speed, prop wash, and breaking crests in storms. Spray is lit by the sun and sky, not unlit white.
- **Rain and weather:** instanced rain streaks angled by the wind, rain impacts on the water (ripple normals), lightning (sky flash plus a brief directional light), and water droplets on the camera lens in storms (a screen-space effect that can be turned off).

### 2.6 Models (Blender scripts, hard-surface)
- **Boats:** hull, deckhouse, windows, railings, mast with radar and lights, tow bitt, fenders, liferings, and hull numbers. Bevel edges. Hull below the waterline is antifouling red. Plausible dimensions from section 4.
- **Tow targets:** a sailboat, a fishing trawler, a motor yacht, a steel barge, and 20 ft / 40 ft shipping containers.
- **Rescue objects:**
  - Person in water: lifejacket orange, head and arms, and a bobbing animation.
  - Canopied life raft.
- **World:**
  - Harbor piers, bollards, cranes, and a fuel dock.
  - Houses.
  - Three lighthouses with rotating beams at night.
  - Navigation buoys: red/green channel markers with lights.
  - Rocky islands with breaking surf.
- **Units:** 1 unit = 1 meter everywhere. Models face +Z, Y is up, and each boat model includes named empties marking: tow point (stern), bow cleat, propeller, rudder, searchlight, and helm camera position.

## 3. Tech & physics architecture

### 3.1 Stack
- Vite plus vanilla JS modules.
- Three.js for rendering.
- Rapier (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) for rigid bodies, collisions (boat vs rocks, piers, and other boats), and joints.
- Physics runs at a **fixed 60 Hz** timestep, independent of frame rate, with rendering interpolated between physics states.

### 3.2 Wave model (`src/ocean/Waves.js`, the single source of truth)
- **Wave list:** the ocean is a list of Gerstner wave components `{direction, wavelength, amplitude, steepness, speed}`, generated from the current **sea state** (section 7) using a seeded RNG. Directions spread ±40° around the wind direction. Wavelengths are distributed log-uniformly across a sea-state-dependent range. Speed follows deep-water dispersion: `c = sqrt(g·λ / 2π)`.
- **Transitions:** when the weather changes, the wave list interpolates over about 60 s. No popping.
- **CPU API:**
  - `heightAt(x, z, t)`: Gerstner waves displace points horizontally, so solve for the undisplaced position with 3 fixed-point iterations: `p0 = p − horizontalDisplacement(p0)`.
  - `normalAt(x, z, t)`.
  - `velocityAt(x, z, t)`: orbital velocity from the wave derivatives.
- **GPU:** the same list, passed as uniforms.
- **Test:** CPU `heightAt` compared against a JS port of the exact shader math must agree within 0.02 m.
- **Physics subset:** the physics may evaluate only the 8 largest components for performance, but must use the same components the shader uses. Small ripple waves are visual-only.

### 3.3 Buoyancy (`src/physics/Buoyancy.js`)
- **Voxelization:** at load, voxelize each hull below its deck line into sample points on a 0.3–0.6 m grid (finer for small boats), using inside-mesh raycast tests. Cache the result. Each point stores its local position, the volume it represents `Vᵢ`, and a height `hᵢ`.
- **Per physics step, for each point:**
  - Submerged fraction: `fᵢ = clamp((waterY − (pY − hᵢ/2)) / hᵢ, 0, 1)`.
  - Buoyant force (upward, applied at the point): `F = ρ·g·Vᵢ·fᵢ`, with `ρ = 1025`.
  - Water drag, using velocity relative to the water (including `velocityAt` orbital velocity plus current), with **anisotropic** coefficients in the boat's local frame. Longitudinal is low. Lateral is high (keel and hull side resistance). Vertical is high (damps heave and pitch). Formula: `F = −½·ρ·Cd·A·|v|·v` per axis.
- **Displacement check:** total volume must be scaled so that at rest on calm water the boat sits at its **design waterline** within ±5 cm. This is tested.
- **Slamming:** when a point's downward velocity into the water exceeds about 3 m/s, add an extra impulse, spawn spray, paint foam, and play a hull slam sound scaled by impact.
- **Budget:** 64 points for the player boat, 32 per tow target, 8 per container, and 400 total per step at most.

### 3.4 Propulsion & steering (`src/physics/Propulsion.js`)
- **Throttle** is a lever from −1 to +1 that stays where you set it. W/S move it in steps. A throttle change takes 0.6 s to reach RPM (engine inertia).
- **Propeller thrust** is applied at the prop empty:
  `T = throttle · T_max · submerged(prop) · (1 − v_fwd / v_prop_max)`.
  - Reverse runs at 45% efficiency.
  - If the prop point is out of the water (stern lifted by a wave), thrust drops toward 0 and RPM spikes (sound). This is **ventilation** and must be audible and visible.
- **Rudder** force is applied at the rudder empty:
  - Lift `L = ½·ρ·A_r·Cl(α)·v_flow²`, with `Cl = 2π·α` and stall above 35°.
  - Flow speed includes prop wash: `v_flow² = v_fwd² + k_wash·|T| / (ρ·A_prop)`.
  - The result is that you can steer at low speed by blipping the throttle, just like a real boat, and steering is weak in reverse.
- **Planing** (Kestrel only): a hydrodynamic lift on bottom points that grows with forward speed², so above about 14 kn the bow rises and wetted area and drag drop. The transition must feel like a distinct moment.
- **Wind:** force `½·ρ_air·Cd·A_windage·v_rel²` applied at the windage center (above the waterline), so the boat heels and drifts downwind. Bigger deckhouses catch more wind.
- **Current:** a slow procedural current field, stronger in channels between islands.

### 3.5 Stability, flooding & damage (`src/physics/Hull.js`)
- **Stability** emerges from buoyancy distribution; don't fake it. The center of mass is set per boat. A boat that heels past its **capsize angle** (section 4) with no recovery for 2 s capsizes: it rolls over, the run fails, and it floats inverted.
- **Flooding:**
  - Water enters when deck-edge points are submerged, at a rate proportional to submerged depth, plus hull leaks from damage.
  - Flood water is extra mass distributed low in the hull. It also shifts toward the low side (free-surface effect), which reduces stability. This is the mechanic that makes overloaded boats in storms dangerous.
  - Pumps remove water at their rated capacity.
  - Above 60% of reserve buoyancy flooded, the boat founders and sinks.
- **Hull integrity** runs 100 → 0. Collision impulses above a threshold reduce it, and grounding on rocks or shallows damages it heavily. Below 60% it leaks at a rate that grows as integrity drops. Repairs happen at port.

### 3.6 Tow line (`src/physics/TowLine.js`)
- **Physics:** a spring-damper constraint with slack, between the tug's tow point and the target's bow cleat.
  - If distance `d > L` (the current line length): tension `T = k·(d − L) + c·ḋ`, clamped ≥ 0. Apply equal and opposite forces at both points.
  - If `d ≤ L`, the line is slack, with tension 0.
  - `k` is set so the line stretches about 15% at breaking strength (synthetic line).
- **Snatch loads:** going from slack to taut at speed produces tension spikes. This is the danger of towing in waves, and it's what the **winch auto-tension** upgrade reduces by paying line out on spikes.
- **Breaking:** the line breaks if `T > breaking strength` for more than 0.25 s, or instantly above 1.5× breaking strength. When it breaks: a loud snap sound, the two halves whip back, and the target drifts.
- **Winch:** pays out or hauls in line (the `L` rate) with Q/Z. Line length is 10–120 m.
- **Visuals:** a Verlet rope with 40 nodes pinned at both ends, sagging under gravity, lying on or sinking into the water (a floating line rides the surface). It's rendered as a tube with rope-lay normals. When taut it straightens and lifts out of the water, dripping spray. It must read clearly at every distance.
- **Chains:** the tug can tow up to 3 containers daisy-chained (each has its own line).

### 3.7 Collision
Rapier colliders for:
- Boats: a convex hull decomposition, 3–6 pieces.
- Piers, rocks, and islands: static trimesh or heightfield.
- Tow targets.

Seabed depth comes from a depth map. When hull bottom points go below the seabed, the boat grounds: friction, damage, and a scraping sound.

## 6. World: "The Grey Reach"

- **Size:** a 6 × 6 km play region of open sea, with the ocean continuing past the edge. A soft boundary: past 5 km from center, radio says you're outside the service area, and past 7 km the job fails and you're towed home (for a fee).
- **Ports:**
  - **Kettle Harbor** (home): a sheltered harbor behind a breakwater with calm water inside. Fuel, repairs, shipyard (buy boats and upgrades), job board, and boat switching.
  - **Pellow Point:** a fuel dock and small pier under the main lighthouse. Fuel and basic repair only.
  - **Farrow Island station:** remote, facing open sea, with the best storm salvage nearby. Unlocks at 25 reputation (fuel and survivor drop-off).
- **Terrain:**
  - 8–12 granite islands made from noise-based heightmaps, with steep cliffs, spruce on top (instanced), and breaking surf at their bases.
  - Reefs and shallows marked on the chart. The depth map drives water color, surf, and grounding.
- **Navigation aids:**
  - A lit red/green buoyed channel into Kettle Harbor.
  - Three lighthouses with realistic beam sweeps at night.
  - Hazard buoys over reefs.
- **Ambient traffic:**
  - 4–8 AI vessels (ferry, fishing boats) following routes. They make wakes.
  - They're also the source of many jobs: a ferry loses a passenger overboard, a trawler's engine fails.
- **Day/night:** one game day lasts 24 real minutes, with realistic sunrise and sunset colors. Nights are dark: running lights, lighthouse beams, the searchlight, and flares are the light sources.

## 7. Weather

**Sea states** (`src/config/weather.js`), with significant wave height `Hs`:

| State | Wind | Hs | Visibility | Rain | Rescue call rate | Payout multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm | 5 kn | 0.3 m | 8 km | none | low | 1.0× |
| Moderate | 15 kn | 1.2 m | 5 km | light chance | normal | 1.2× |
| Rough | 25 kn | 2.5 m | 2 km | showers | high | 1.6× |
| Gale | 38 kn | 4.5 m | 800 m | heavy | very high | 2.2× |
| Storm | 50 kn | 7 m | 400 m | torrential, lightning | extreme | 3.0× |

- **Cycles:** weather follows a seeded Markov chain with persistence, and storms build and ease over 10–20 min. It's a **forecast** system: the radio and job board show the next 2 game days. Storms arrive from one side of the map as a visible wall of darker sky and rain.
- **Gameplay effects by boat:**
  - Kestrel is dangerous above Rough.
  - Marlin is dangerous above Gale.
  - Bulwark can work a Storm.
- **Survival:** the sea temperature is 11 °C. A person in the water survives about 20 min in Calm and about 10 min in Storm (wind chill and wave exposure), interpolated between.

## 8. Jobs & career

### 8.1 Job types

| Job | Description | Success condition | Base pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Person in water** | 1–3 people overboard from a ferry or trawler, drifting with wind and current, found by approximate position, flares, and whistles (spatial audio) | Approach within 4 m at under 2 kn and hold for 3 s per person (E), then deliver to any port before hypothermia | $300 + $250 per survivor |
| **Life raft** | 4–8 people in a drifting canopied raft | Same, 2 s per person. Small boats may need multiple trips; the raft keeps drifting | $200 + $180 per survivor |
| **Crew transfer** | A sinking vessel with 2–5 crew aboard | Hold alongside within 2.5 m, side by side, for 5 s per person, before it sinks (a visible timer that speeds up in bad weather) | $400 + $300 per crew |
| **Disabled tow** | A vessel with engine failure | Tow it into a port's harbor zone | 15% of vessel value × condition (0–1) |
| **Swamped vessel** | A vessel awash (half flooded) | Connect the pump hose (hold within 8 m for 20 s, E), then tow | Tow pay + $800 |
| **Cargo recovery** | 3–6 containers drifting after a spill | Tow each to Kettle Harbor; the tug can chain up to 3 | $350 each |

- **Weather multiplier:** pay for rescues and cargo is multiplied by the section 7 weather multiplier. Tow jobs use **half** the multiplier bonus: `1 + (mult − 1)/2`.
- **Vessel values:**
  - Sailboat $6k.
  - Trawler $14k.
  - Motor yacht $30k (requires reputation 40).
  - Barge $60k (requires reputation 70; realistically only the Bulwark can tow it).
- **Condition** starts at 1.0 and drops with collisions, grounding, and flooding during the tow.
- **Failure:** survivors lost, the target sinks, or you abandon the job. Failure costs reputation (−10 per life lost, −5 per lost tow) and pays nothing.

### 8.2 Reputation (0–100)
- **Gains:** +5 per survivor delivered and +3 per successful tow.
- **Unlocks:** Farrow Island at 25, yacht tows at 40, barge contracts at 70.
- Reputation also increases how many job offers appear at once (3 at 0, up to 6 at 60+).

### 8.3 Radio & job board
- **Radio:** mayday calls arrive as text messages in the radio panel with a burst of static and a beep. Each shows the vessel name, the nature of the emergency, an approximate position (an uncertainty circle on the chart that shrinks as you get closer), and the time pressure.
- **Accepting:** you can accept a job from the radio or from the job board at port. Only one active job at a time, but a mayday can be accepted mid-job; the current job then continues its clock.
- **Offer rate:** new offers appear every 1–4 game minutes, scaled by the weather call rate.

### 8.4 Autopilot & time compression
After the autopilot upgrade:
- **T** steers to the selected chart waypoint.
- In open water with no job action needed within 300 m and sea state Moderate or lower, time can be compressed up to 4×.
- Compression turns off automatically on any radio call, any object within 300 m, rising sea state, or player input.

### 8.5 Economy

**Costs:**
- **Starting money:** $1,500.
- **Fuel:** $1.20/L. A full Marlin refuel costs about $120.
- **Repairs:** $12 per 1% integrity (Marlin), $5 (Kestrel), $40 (Bulwark).
- **Tow-home rescue** (fail by leaving the map or sinking): $1,000 plus repairs.

**Upgrades** (equipment is shared across boats unless marked per-boat):

### 8.6 Save
Save to localStorage: money, reputation, boats owned and their upgrades, damage state, time of day, weather state, and seed. Autosave at every port visit and every 2 minutes. The title screen has "Continue" and "New Career".

- The objective, with distance and bearing to target.
- A time pressure bar (hypothermia or sinking).
- Survivors aboard as a count out of capacity.

**Bottom right:** the radio log (last 3 messages, fading).

**Minimap:**
- Chart-style: paper color, ink coastlines, depth contours.
- Shows the player, targets, and the job uncertainty circle.
- With radar, it also shows radar returns.

**Other screens:**
- **Chart (M):** full-screen nautical-chart style with coastlines, depth shading, buoys, ports, the job circle, waypoints, and the current weather and forecast.
- **Job board / shipyard / repair:** chart-paper cards with ink text and brass dividers.
- **Waypoint markers** in the world are small, precise, and flat. No glowing beams.

## 10. Audio (Web Audio, all synthesized, `src/audio/`)

**Engine:**
- A layered sawtooth plus filtered noise whose pitch and filter follow RPM.
- Each boat gets a different timbre: Kestrel is an outboard whine, Marlin a diesel, Bulwark a deep slow diesel.
- Ventilation makes the RPM spike and the sound thin.

**Sea:**
- Continuous wave wash: noise shaped by the local wave height under the boat.
- Hull slaps and slams.
- Spray hiss at speed.
- Surf roar near shores.

**Weather:** wind (a band-passed noise howl that rises with wind speed), rain (dense noise layers, louder in the helm camera on the roof), and thunder (delayed after lightning by distance).

**Tow line:** creaks above 70% tension, a winch motor sound, and a violent snap when it breaks.

**Rescue:**
- Survivors' whistles as 3D spatialized audio, the key to finding them in fog.
- The flare launch and its hiss.

**Radio:** static bursts, a squelch tail, and alert beeps. No voice acting.

**UI:** mechanical clicks and soft chimes.

**Mix:** master, SFX, ambience, and radio volume sliders. The mix ducks when the radio speaks.

## 11. Game flow

1. **Title screen:** a slow camera over Kettle Harbor at dawn with the sea in motion. "Continue" (if a save exists), "New Career", "Settings", "Controls". Title typography in IBM Plex Sans Condensed with the brass accent.
2. **New career:** a short intro radio message from the harbormaster, then a guided first job (Calm weather, a disabled sailboat tow near the harbor) that teaches throttle, rudder, prop-wash steering, attaching the line, and winch control, through contextual prompts only (no tutorial popups over the action).
3. **Main loop:** take jobs → go out → complete or fail → return to port → refuel, repair, upgrade → watch the forecast → decide whether to go out into the storm.
4. **No "game over":** failures cost money and reputation. Capsizing or sinking ends the current job and gets you towed home (with the fee plus repairs). You can go negative on money; at −$5,000 the harbormaster covers you once, then the career ends ("Bankruptcy", shown with stats).
5. **Pause menu:** Resume, Settings, Controls, Save & Quit.
6. **Settings:**
   - Quality preset (Low/Medium/High/Ultra).
   - Individual toggles for post effects, lens droplets, and shadows.
   - Horizon-lock camera on/off.
   - Units (knots / km/h).
   - Audio sliders.
   - Invert camera.

## 12. Performance targets

- **High preset:** 60 fps at 1080p on a mid-range gaming laptop (GTX 1650 class).
- **Low preset:** 40 fps or better on integrated graphics (Iris Xe class).
- **Physics:** under 4 ms per step with the Bulwark towing 3 containers in a Storm.
- **Draw calls:** under 300 (instancing for trees, rocks, rain, spray, and buoys).
- **Debug overlay:** F3 shows FPS, frame time, physics time, draw calls, buoyancy points active, current sea state, and wave count.

## 15. Guided first job (tutorial) script

1. **Harbormaster radio:** "Sailboat *Wren* has lost her engine just outside the breakwater. Take the Marlin out and bring her in."
2. **Prompt:** "W to set throttle." It shows until the throttle is above 20%.
3. **Prompt at the harbor mouth:** "Steer with A/D. At low speed, a blip of throttle helps the rudder bite." This triggers if the player is steering at under 3 kn.
4. **Near the Wren:** "Come within 8 m of her bow at under 3 knots. Space to pass the line."
5. **After attaching:** "Q/Z to pay out and haul in. Keep tension in the green: jerks can part the line."
6. **At the harbor zone:** "Space to cast off. Job complete." It pays out, then a prompt suggests the job board.

The weather is locked to Calm and daylight until the tutorial is complete.

## 17. Verification (`npm run verify` < 3 min total)

### 17.1 `npm run test:physics` (headless Node + Rapier, no rendering, runs faster than real time)
- **For each boat:**
  - Design waterline (±5 cm).
  - Top speed (within ±15%).
  - Acceleration time.
  - Stopping distance.
  - Turning circle.
  - Natural roll period (release from 15° heel in calm and measure the oscillation period).
  - Capsize angle (static heel test).
- **Tow:** Marlin + trawler tow speed; the snatch-load spike ratio in Rough; line breaking at the rated tension; the auto-tension upgrade cutting peak tension by 30% or more.
- **Waves:** CPU/GPU wave agreement.
- **Output:** a pass/fail table.

### 17.2 `npm run verify` (Playwright)
1. Build, start the preview, and open the game in headless Chromium (WebGL via SwiftShader is fine; fps is **not** checked here).
2. Run `test:physics`.
3. **Browser smoke test** (about 60–90 s):
   - Start a new career.
   - Throttle up for 20 s and check that speed increases and the boat's Y position varies with waves (it's floating, not fixed).
   - Run the tutorial job via the autopilot helper through to attachment.
   - Toggle every sea state with F6 and check that there are no errors.
   - Open the chart, job board, and shipyard.
   - Take screenshots to `verify-shots/`.
4. Fail on any page error, console error, NaN in the physics state, a boat below −5 m without sinking state, or a failed physics test.

### 17.3 `npm run sim:economy`
A pure-JS simulation of career progression using average job durations and payouts per sea state, plus a simple player policy (buy the next upgrade in the list order when affordable, work storms only with a suitable boat). It prints money over time and unlock times and fails if they fall outside the section 8.5 pacing targets by more than 25%.

### 17.4 Screenshot review
At each milestone, check these screenshots against section 2 and write one line per screenshot in `PROGRESS.md` about what looks wrong. Fix anything that clearly breaks the art direction before committing.
